> [!Note] Chapter2 problem4
Prove the converse to Runge's theorem: if $K$ is a compact set whose complement if not connected, then there exists a function $f$ holomorphic in a neighborhood of $K$ which cannot be approximated uniformly by polynomial on $K$.
[Hint: Pick a point $z_{0}$ in a bounded component of $K^c$, and let $f(z) = 1/(z - z_0)$. If $f$ can be approximated uniformly by polynomials on $K$, show that there exists a polynomial $p$ such that $|(z - z_0)p(z) - 1| < 1$. Use the maximum modulus principle (Chapter 3) to show that this inequality continues to hold for all $z$ in the component of $K^c$ that contains $z_{0}$]

Proof.
Assume conversely, there is a series of polynomials $P_{n}\rightrightarrows f= \frac{1}{z-z_{0}}$. For all $\varepsilon$, there exsits $N$, such that for all $n>N$, $\sup_{z\in K}\lvert P_{n}​	-f \rvert<\varepsilon$​	
Due to $K$ is bounded, we have at least one $P_{n}$ $\left\lvert  (z-z_{0})\left( P(z)- \frac{1}{z-z_{0}} \right)  \right\rvert<1,\: \forall z\in K$
$\lim_{z\to z_{0}}\left\lvert  (z-z_{0})\left( P(z)- \frac{1}{z-z_{0}} \right)  \right\rvert=1$
Now, consider the connected component S of $K^c$ that contains $z_0$. Since S is open and bounded, its closure $\bar{S}$ is compact. The function $g(z) = (z - z_0)P(z) - 1$ is holomorphic on S (as P is a polynomial). By the maximum modulus principle, the modulus of a holomorphic function does not attain its maximum in an open set unless constant. Thus, $|g(z)|$ must attain its maximum on the boundary ∂S. Therefore, $\lvert g(z_{1}) \rvert>1$ for some $z_{1}\in \partial S$
However,$z_{1}\in K$, $|(z_{1} - z)p(z_{1}) - 1|>1$, comtradicts to $\lvert (z-z_{0})P(z)-1​	 \rvert<1$, which completes the proof.

$$
\begin{align}

\end{align}
$$
By Residue Formula, we choose curve $\gamma _R$, and $\int_{\gamma_{R}} \frac{\cos z}{z^{2}+a^{2}}dz=2\pi i\:res_{z=ia}f$

We calculate integral on the semicircle. 
$$
\begin{align}

> [!Note] 13
Suppose $f(z)$ is holomorphic in a punctured disc $D_r(z_0) - \{ z_0 \}$. Suppose also that
>$$\begin{align*}
> |f(z)| \leq A |z - z_0 |^{-1 + \epsilon}
> \end{align*}$$
for some $\epsilon > 0$, and all $z$ near $z_0$. Show that the singularity of $f$ at $z_0$ is removable.

Proof.
Let $g(z)= f(z)(z-z_{0})$. By $|f(z)| \leq A |z - z_0 |^{-1 + \epsilon}$, we have $\lvert g(z) \rvert\leq A\lvert z-z_{0} \rvert^{\varepsilon}$. Since $\lim_{z\to z_{0}}A\lvert z-z_{0} \rvert^{\varepsilon}=0$, $z_{0}$ is either a removable singularity, or a holomorphic point of g.
Define
$$
\bar{g}(z)=
\begin{cases}
g(z)\quad z\neq z_{0} \\
0 \quad z=z_{0} 
\end{cases}
$$​It's clear that $\lim_{z\to z_{0}}\bar{g}(z)=\lim_{z\to z_{0}}g(z)$, which gives $\lim_{z\to z_{0}}f(z)=\lim_{z\to z_{0}} \frac{g(z)}{z-z_{0}}=\lim_{z\to z_{0}} \frac{\bar{g}(z)}{z-z_{0}}$. From the taylor formula of holomorphic function, it follows that $\bar{g}(z)=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}a_{i}(z-z_{0})^{i}$ with $a_{0}=0$. Therefore, $\lim_{z\to z_{0}} \frac{\bar{g}(z)}{z-z_{0}}=a_{1}$, which proves that $z_{0}$ is removable zero of $f$.
