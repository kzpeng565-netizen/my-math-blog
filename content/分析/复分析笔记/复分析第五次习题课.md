## 1. Basic Information
Harmonic function
Definition
domain $\Omega \subset \mathbb{R}^{2}$ or $\mathbb{C}$. If $u(x,y)\text{or}(u(z))$ is $C^{2}(\Omega)$, and satisfies Laplace equation: $\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial x^{2}}+\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial y^{2}}=1$
$\mathcal{H}(\Omega)=\{ \text{harmonic functions in }\Omega\}$

Note:
1.  $f(z)=u(z)+v(z)i$ is holomorphic , we can infer $u,v$ is harmonic
2. when $\Omega$ is simple connected, $u$ and its conjugate harmonic function $v$ can form a holomorphic function

Property
Composition: 
$u(z)\in \mathcal{H}^{2}(\Omega),f(z\in \mathcal{O}(D))$, with $\mathrm{Im}(f)\subset\Omega$, then $u(f(z))\in \mathcal{H}^{2}(\Omega)$

## 2. Property of harmonic function
## 2.1 average value principle
Let $u(z)$ be a harmonic function on $\{ |z-z_{0}|<R\}$, then
$$
u(z_{0}) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi} u(z_{0}+re^{i\theta}) \, d\theta \quad \forall0<r<R
$$
Proof
$f(z_{0})= \frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\lvert \xi-z_{0} \rvert=R} \frac{f(z_{0}+re^{i\theta})}{\xi-z_{0}}d\xi= \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}  \frac{f(z_{0}+re^{i\theta})}{\cancel{\xi-z_{0}}} \, \cancel{re^{i\theta}}​	​d\theta$
$f(z_{0})=u+iv$ and compare the Real part and imagine part, we obtain the result

## 2.2 extremum principle
Theorem
Let $u(z)$ be a harmonic function on $\Omega$ and it is not constant. Then, $u(z)$
must attain its maximum only on $\partial\Omega$

# 3. Possion Formula

Schwarz formula
$f(z)=u(z)+iv(z)$ is holomorphic on $D_{R}(0)$ , and continuous on $C_{R}(0)$
then,
$$
f(z)= \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\lvert \xi \rvert =R} \frac{\xi-z}{\xi+z}u(\xi) \frac{d\xi}{\xi}+i\mathrm{Im}f(0)
$$
Proof.
By Cauchy Formula, we have 
$$f(z)= \frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\lvert \xi \rvert =R} \frac{f(\xi)}{\xi-z}d\xi= \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi} \frac{\xi f(\xi)}{\xi-z} \, d\theta $$
From Cauchy Theorem, it follows that
$$
\int_{\lvert \xi \rvert =R} \frac{f(\xi)}{\xi- \frac{\xi \bar{\xi}}{z}}d\xi=0 \quad \text{since} \frac{R^{2}}{\bar{z}}  \text{is out of the disc}
$$
$$
f(z)= \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\lvert \xi \rvert =R} \frac{\xi f(\xi)+z\overline{f(\xi)}}{\xi-z}d\theta=\frac{1}{2\pi }\int_{\lvert \xi \rvert=R} \frac{\xi+z}{\xi-z}u(\xi)d\theta+ \underbrace{\frac{i}{2\pi}\int_{\lvert \xi \rvert=R}v(\xi)d\theta}_{i\mathrm{Im}(f(0))}
$$
